Research

I study how operating organizations, mostly in health care, absorb change that comes from outside them: a shifting workforce, a new policy or payment rule, a new technology placed into a service line. The work combines panel econometrics where the question is about consequence with simulation and optimization where the question is about design.

Healthcare operations & workforce Buyer–supplier relationships & innovation Public policy & sustainability in operations AI placement in service processes Causal inference methods
Dissertation
How Health Care Delivery Organizations Absorb External Change: Workforce, Policy, and Technology

Three kinds of external change, one published base study and three essays. The same two conditions govern each case: whether the organization is operating under high demand, and how far the affected groups differ in their capacity to adapt. Read together, the essays rank three responses from weakest to strongest: a single institution's own operating decisions, system-level policy support, and placing technology where it actually binds.
